# LEVERAGE: CINDER CITY — Production Bible

**Working draft 0.1 · 25 November 2026 · Discovery and initial research**  
**Status:** Proposed design; not owner-approved, implemented, licensed or device-tested. This is the first instalment, not the complete pre-production bible.

## 1. Authority and decision log

The supplied A–Z master brief governs its archived legacy passages. No prior project is required to proceed. Its fixed foundation is Marcus; his kidnapped brother; the Blue Ledger; debt, family, crew, corruption, corporate interests, betrayal and consequential choices; the six-act story spine; two Chapter One Ledger outcomes; five combat disciplines; the specified combat mapping; and the comic-noir identity. The brief requires an expansive world, a 60-minute minimum target for a first main-story playthrough, and iPhone-first Xogot/GDScript production. These are requirements, not verified implementation or performance.

**Owner decision:** Cinder City is an international blend. **Owner delegation:** recommend the creative direction throughout; it must feel advanced, solid, sophisticated, thrilling, chaotic, dramatic, fun and engaging. Major creative recommendations below remain **proposed**, not historically established canon or approved designs.

**Proposed direction from conversation:** an invented port metropolis shaped by migration and trade; Marcus loves its people but has considered leaving; the brothers remain connected but conceal painful truths. Specific real-world inspirations should be researched respectfully and developed into an original location.

**Document conflict:** opening order in the A–Z front section places studio identity and title before the roughly 30-second cinematic; the archived older timeline places the title after its cinematic beats. Front section governs pending owner review. **Unresolved historical facts:** the brief does not specify the brother's name, the full sixteen identities, named factions or exact historic Ledger outcome details. ## 2. Creative north star

A man looking for his brother discovers that information is a debt someone always intends to collect. The city is dangerous but worth caring about: a place of night-shift food stalls, flooded service roads, overlapping languages, social obligations and institutional money. It should be exciting to inhabit, not a backdrop composed only of misery.

**Player promise:** enter a location with a lead, read people and space, choose how to intervene, then deal with a concrete consequence. A fight may destroy an irreplaceable recording; bargaining may protect a witness but put another contact in debt. Escalation can be chaotic, but input, threats and objectives must remain legible.

**Four experience pillars**
1. **People are the stakes:** every major conspiracy beat touches a relationship, not only an abstract institution.
2. **Leverage is playable:** evidence, trust, debts, threats and favours create options and costs. Knowledge reaches a character only through a recorded event.
3. **Action has texture:** movement, five fighting disciplines, firearms and vehicles have distinct feedback, credible recovery and readable enemy intent.
4. **The city answers:** witnesses, businesses, factions and police react in authored, understandable ways; changes survive save/load where consequential.

## 3. Proposed canon: place, cast and power

### Cinder City
An invented coastal trading city whose old port districts, hillside neighbourhoods, market streets and financial towers overlap. Migration and commerce shape its food, speech, architecture and music. The inspirations are not substitute cultures or permission to copy specific landmarks; cultural research and consultant review remain open dependencies. The six initial districts are anchors rather than maximums:

- **Hearthline (residential):** walk-up apartments, family businesses and an elevated rail approach; Marcus's mechanic shop and family home.
- **Glassward (commercial):** bank concourse, civic offices, corporate atrium and loading access beneath polished frontage.
- **Violet Mile (nightlife):** music venues, service lanes, licensed security and informal information markets.
- **Foundry Reach (industrial):** vehicle repair yards, workshops, power distribution and restricted records storage.
- **Low Cross (criminal territory):** small businesses under competing protection demands; a contested public market.
- **Breakwater (docks):** container lanes, customs offices, ferry infrastructure, warehouses and the final exchange site.

Weather, signage, landmarks, population and interiors are design targets, not measured assets. These names are proposed.

They overlap but do not form a single all-controlling conspiracy. Names and structures are proposed.

### Ledger and causal timeline

The Blue Ledger is an incomplete encrypted reconciliation record, with transaction IDs, dates, shell-company references and short authorisation codes. It links suspicious payments to altered cargo declarations; it does not itself prove who ordered a kidnapping. Keira can authenticate some entries against independent records. Missing pages and ambiguous codes create risks of false accusation.

1. A legitimate shipment is redirected using a forged authorisation; a driver and an innocent contractor become exposed.
2. Eli notices the discrepancy and takes a partial copy to protect a witness. His earlier signature on a routine form is exploited to portray him as responsible.
3. Pavel sells part of the disputed obligation. Darius's crew is pressured to locate Eli; security separately seeks the copy.
4. Eli calls Marcus, is seized in a staged abduction and moved to a warehouse. His abductors have conflicting instructions; his safety is not guaranteed.
5. Marcus gathers independent clues and learns that someone close to him concealed a relevant fact. The identity and precise staging of betrayal must be scripted and reviewed, not treated as an already approved historical fact.
6. At Breakwater, evidence, witness safety and the brother's release are negotiated or fought over. The Ledger decision alters who can act next.

**Knowledge rule:** a discovered clue changes Marcus's knowledge only. Other people learn it through a call, witnessed action, explicitly shared evidence or authored propagation event. Each story event must name sender, recipient, timestamp/trigger and resulting knowledge; offscreen omniscience is prohibited.

## 4. Proposed campaign framework

The following is a mission skeleton, not the required complete script. Segment timing is an **untested design estimate**. Distinct actions and dramatically necessary conversations determine actual length; no claim of 60-minute playtime is made until first-time playtests.

| Act | Mission / compact stages | Player purpose and escalation | Persistent consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Last Call:** family shop → interrupted call → search the pickup point → first confrontation | Learn movement, phone and interaction through the disappearance; talk or fight for a witness lead. | Witness trust and public violence recorded. |
| I | **A Debt in Pieces:** visit Pavel → verify a payment → meet Darius | Discover that the debt has multiple claimants; negotiate payment, favour or exposure. | Debt obligation and crew response recorded. |
| II | **A Blue Thread:** inspect delivery route → recover partial Ledger → compare a manifest | Separate a document's claims from proof; establish a source and chain of custody. | Evidence possession and integrity recorded. |
| II | **Two Doors:** contact Keira → approach the records room → decide where the copy goes | Choose a nonviolent, stealth or forceful entry; enter one of two Chapter One outcome paths. | Ledger path and recipients saved. |
| III | **After the Noise:** respond to consequences → protect or relocate Sade → escape an interception | Consequences manifest through changing access, threats and calls. | Witness safety, identification and police attention recorded. |
| III | **No Safe Favour:** help Tessa or repay Darius → investigate an information leak | An owed favour changes access without blocking essential progress. | Shop access, crew debt and a source of assistance recorded. |
| IV | **The Signature:** locate Eli's authorisation → question Keira and Celia → confront a trusted contact | Distinguish a forged authorisation from a real compromised signature; reveal the betrayal. | Knowledge, trust and evidence provenance recorded. |
| IV | **Inside the Transfer:** enter Foundry Reach → find transfer logs → extract a corroborating record | Combine stealth, conversation and combat with alternatives when a clue is missed. | Proof reliability and security alert recorded. |
| V | **Terms of Release:** gather allies → prepare a route → negotiate with competing claimants | Choose what to protect and what to reveal; no perfect payout. | Promises and participants in the climax recorded. |
| V | **Breakwater:** reach the exchange → rescue or bargain → confrontation and escape | Earlier allies can provide a route, warning or distraction; combat is not the only resolution. | Eli's condition, evidence exposure and faction response recorded. |
| VI | **The Cost:** return to family → meet remaining contacts → aftermath | Resolve immediate rescue and show the consequences for family, witnesses, the city and Marcus. | Ending state retained for continuity. |

### Two proposed Chapter One Ledger outcomes

These do **not** claim to reproduce the unspecified historical outcomes. Owner confirmation is required before either is canon.

- **Controlled disclosure:** Marcus gives Lena and Keira a partial copy with provenance. They agree not to publish until independently checked. Benefit: an external safeguard and later verification; cost: security discovers a leak and a source becomes vulnerable. Save `ledger_path=disclosure`, recipient knowledge, evidence integrity and witness exposure.
- **Private bargain:** Marcus gives Celia exclusive access in exchange for a time-limited lead on Eli. Benefit: a route and immediate leverage; cost: Meridian can suppress or manipulate the record. Save `ledger_path=bargain`, recipient knowledge, promise and evidence integrity. Marcus retains an incomplete trace so essential investigation remains possible.

Both routes must have independently authored scenes, explicit trigger predicates, a saved branch state, failure recovery, and reload tests. The path is distinct from the later choice of how much evidence to expose. Neither creates a guaranteed consequence-free rescue.

### Sample opening scene — proposed, not final script

*Rain rattles the shop shutter. Marcus checks a car battery while the phone vibrates against a workbench. Eli's voice is warm but distracted.*

**ELI:** You still owe me for the window.  
**MARCUS:** Ten years and you're still keeping accounts?  
**ELI:** Somebody in this family has to.  
*Marcus smiles, then hears a vehicle door slam on Eli's end.*  
**MARCUS:** Where are you?  
**ELI:** If anybody asks about me tonight, you haven't seen me.  
**MARCUS:** Eli—  
*A scrape, a muffled voice, the call cuts out.*

The player inspects the call location and chooses how to approach a witness. The witness can later be reached through another lead if missed. The complete script, player responses, staging, subtitle timing and interruption states are outstanding.

## 5. Systems: proposed design boundaries

### Leverage and evidence
Each evidence item has a stable ID, source, acquisition event, reliability, integrity, related entities, known recipients and allowed actions. Choices show intent and an understandable likely risk, not a guaranteed forecast. Core story leads have at least one recovery route. Track individual trust, faction hostility, police identification, debt and evidence exposure separately; expose consequences through behaviour and dialogue rather than unexplained scores.

### Combat and input
Preserve **X punch, Y heavy, A kick, B dodge**, with B contextual outside combat. A state-aware router prevents dialogue, phone, menu, cinematic and combat handlers from consuming the same touch. The five core styles—Brawler, Kickboxing, Grappling, Drunken Fighting, Weapon Fighting—need authored timing, stamina, range, tells, counters and animation lists. Avoid turning Drunken Fighting into unreliable player controls. Group pressure should come from positioning and readable attacks rather than simultaneous unavoidable hits; firearms require mobile-specific aiming and encounter balance. Numerical timing, targeting and tuning are untested.

**Sample encounter:** two untrained rushers and one cautious lookout in a market service lane. The lookout may call for help after a readable warning; a player can interrupt, negotiate before combat, withdraw or fight. Breaking a recording device is possible only with clear feedback and an alternate path to essential information. Later encounters introduce defensive specialists and mixed threats, rather than just increasing health.

### City, phones and persistence
The phone handles calls, message history, evidence and navigation; incoming calls follow explicit state and interruption rules. Mission-critical calls have a recovery path after decline or app suspension. Police escalation requires a report, severity, identification and a fair search/escape process. Save version, checkpoint, mission stage, Ledger path, evidence and knowledge, relationships, debts, calls, world changes and settings. Validate and preserve the last known good save; migration and interruption tests are required.

### Pacing and accessibility
Design many purposeful short stages, generally 2–5 minutes, allowing longer scenes when drama needs them. Instrument first-time and experienced playtests, report median and range, and revise without filler. Provide scalable subtitles, contrast, touch targets, camera sensitivity, inversion and reduced shake; final details require device usability testing. Accessibility, difficulty and input remapping need full specifications before implementation.

### Initial recommendations and trade-offs

- **iPhone workflow — provisional test:** use GDScript and standard Godot project formats. Alternative: desktop-led asset preparation followed by import, if the owner has that workflow available. Advantage: aligns with documented iPhone editor restriction. Risk: complex character and animation authoring may require external production; do not assume that pipeline exists. Acceptance: import one rigged animated character and edit/run on the actual installed Xogot build. **Approval:** final production pipeline and any external collaborators.
- **Combat design — research and prototype after approval:** adapt the principle of readable counterplay and environmental opportunities, not a copy of Sifu's systems. Alternative: simpler context-action combat. Richer interactions support expressive encounters but increase animation, AI and touch-UX cost. Test touch accuracy, player comprehension, hit windows and frame time on both target devices. **Approval:** final combat depth and resource plan.
- **City population — hypothesis:** use authored nearby agents and cheaper distant representations instead of full simulation for everyone. Alternative: entirely authored sparse encounters. The former may support variety but creates spawning and continuity risks; validate visible repetition, navigation, CPU and frame time. No count or performance claim has been established. **Approval:** measured scope after representative testing.
