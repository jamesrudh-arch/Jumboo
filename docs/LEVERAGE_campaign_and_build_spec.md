# LEVERAGE: CINDER CITY — Campaign, Systems and Build Specification

**Working draft 0.3 · 25 November 2026**  
**Status:** Proposed; not an approved complete production bible, not implemented or device-tested. Read alongside `LEVERAGE_production_bible_draft.md` and `LEVERAGE_narrative_slice_and_state_design.md`. Names, decisions and scenes below are creative proposals, not recovered historic canon. The brief specifies two Chapter One Ledger outcomes but not their exact historical events; the two proposed paths need owner confirmation before they are locked.

## 1. Narrative continuity resolution

### Who did what, and why

Meridian Logistics legitimately moves cargo through Breakwater. Security chief Victor Rusk used a real subcontractor's credentials to divert a shipment and secure an unlawful payment. He did not direct every corrupt act in the city: intermediary Pavel brokered one of the resulting debts independently, and Darius accepted a collection job without knowing its ultimate client. Executive Celia Ardent authorised aggressive internal containment after learning of irregularities; she did not authorise the kidnapping but bears responsibility for choosing secrecy over investigation.

Eli Vale signed a routine dispatch correction before he knew it would be paired with a false authorisation. Auditor Keira Moss created a reconciliation index to find discrepancies. Eli copied part of it when he realised Omar could be blamed. Darius's people intercepted Eli intending to recover the copy and hold him temporarily; Rusk's security then moved him without Darius's permission. Eli hid a separate code fragment inside an ordinary maintenance tag before capture. The fragment identifies a record, not the warehouse, and cannot solve the story on its own.

Tessa Ward recognised the collector's vehicle outside the garage before the kidnapping. She withheld the sighting because her employee's family owed Pavel money and she feared revealing them. She did not orchestrate the kidnapping. Marcus confronts her in M06, when a second source establishes the vehicle's presence; how he treats her changes her later assistance. This is the principal personal betrayal, with identifiable cause and recoverable consequences.

### Endgame outcomes

Both Chapter One paths remain meaningful in the climax. Disclosure gives Lena and Keira independently retained copies but increases the circle at risk. The bargain provides Celia's route to a controlled exchange but lets Meridian delay or alter access to records. In either case Marcus can obtain an independently corroborated authorisation through EV-06; no route turns one piece of incomplete data into automatic proof.

Eli can be released alive in the main route through negotiation, intervention or a combination. His condition and willingness to testify depend on protection and preparation; avoid a sudden untelegraphed death determined by an obscure score. A failed exchange is a retryable encounter, not a hidden premature ending. The final decision determines whether corroborated records go to Voss, Lena, or a protected staged handover; each carries different exposure and trust consequences. Marcus's decision to leave or stay is an authored aftermath conversation, not a promise to clean up the entire city.

## 2. Mission-to-mission plan: acts III–VI

M01–M04 and two draft branch outcomes are specified in the companion narrative document. The following expands M05–M11. Each stage target of 2–5 minutes is a hypothesis. Dialogue scenes, major fights and investigations may run longer; the campaign's 60-minute minimum must be measured with first-time players, not inferred from estimates.

### M05 — After the Noise

**Start:** M04 commits a Ledger path. **Place:** Violet Mile service entrance and Sade's market. **Cast:** Marcus, Sade, Mina, a Meridian security contact. **Purpose:** make the branch immediately visible and protect a person, not a hidden statistic.

1. Lena's guarded call in the disclosure route, or Celia's dispatch message in the bargain route, identifies a potential threat to Sade. In neither route does an uninformed party magically know her identity.
2. Reach Sade's stall; examine whether the threat is real by comparing a description and delivery receipt. If trust was damaged in M01, Marcus must admit the pressure or secure a neutral intermediary; Sade can refuse him but remains reachable.
3. Escort Sade through the market or arrange Mina's back-room route. An interception can be evaded, negotiated or fought. A readable lookout warning and two exits prevent a forced unfair ambush.
4. Sade chooses a safe location. Checkpoint records her destination and who knows it. If Marcus publicly names her, the new disclosure creates risk only after an explicit propagation event.

**Completion:** Sade relocated or supported in place with a protective arrangement. **Fail/retry:** Sade incapacitated or Marcus defeated returns to the pre-interception checkpoint; no permanent missed-essential-clue lock. **Tests:** both trust states, each Ledger path, no leaked identity without a transfer event, reload after relocation.

**Scene:**  
**SADE:** You said you wouldn't put my name on it.  
**MARCUS:** I said I'd try to keep you out of it.  
**SADE:** That's not the same thing.  
*If Marcus actually promised anonymity, he acknowledges that promise; otherwise the line is “I never promised you silence.” Mina proposes a route but asks Sade—not Marcus—to agree.*

### M06 — No Safe Favour

**Start:** Sade's situation resolved. **Place:** Tessa's garage and Low Cross. **Cast:** Marcus, Tessa, Darius, Omar. **Purpose:** expose Tessa's omission and test the cost of intimidation.

1. The garage is searched; Tessa produces a damaged work order showing an unfamiliar van was parked nearby before the kidnapping.
2. Omar identifies the distinctive repair. Marcus compares the time against Tessa's earlier account. Player chooses to ask privately, accuse publicly or listen before deciding.
3. Tessa admits she recognised a collector and kept quiet to protect her employee. She offers a vehicle or refuses active help according to the confrontation; Marcus can still use a public transport/contact route.
4. Darius asks for repayment if Marcus accepted an earlier favour. If none is owed, Darius offers information for witness protection rather than retroactively creating debt.

**Completion:** location of the dispatch records and Tessa's omission established. **Persistence:** `tessa_trust`, `garage_access`, any actual `debt_pavel`; these are separate values, not a universal morality score. **Test:** no debt is created for a player who refused M02; essentials remain available at low trust.

**Scene:**  
**MARCUS:** You saw that van outside our shop.  
**TESSA:** I saw the plate, yes. I didn't know they'd take him.  
**MARCUS:** You let me go looking blind.  
**TESSA:** I was trying to keep them from going after my employee's family. I should have told you.  
*Reassure, challenge or leave. No response instantly erases the damage; it affects the terms of later help.*

### M07 — The Signature

**Start:** records lead established. **Place:** Glassward public records office. **Cast:** Marcus, Keira, Celia, Voss by call. **Purpose:** distinguish Eli's real signature from the forged authorisation.

Stages: request or infiltrate the records room; compare original correction and altered authorisation; meet Keira; confront Celia with only evidence Marcus possesses. Optional evidence gives Voss stronger grounds to act; a clerk can supply a delayed certified copy if the player loses or damages the first record.

**Key dialogue:**  
**KEIRA:** The signature is Eli's. The instruction beside it isn't.  
**MARCUS:** You can tell?  
**KEIRA:** Different revision, different timestamp. I can show you the edit history. I can't tell you who pressed the key without the server record.  
**CELIA:** I contained a leak. I did not order anyone taken.  
**MARCUS:** You knew someone could be blamed for it.  
**CELIA:** I knew a great deal was about to come out. I chose the company. That choice is mine.

**Completion:** identify the missing server authorisation. **Test:** if EV-06 absent, Celia dialogue must not claim it exists; the alternate route remains available.

### M08 — Inside the Transfer

**Start:** M07 conclusion. **Place:** Foundry Reach power-and-records annex. **Cast:** Marcus, optional Omar or Tessa, Rusk by intercom. **Purpose:** obtain independent corroboration under pressure.

1. Scout ventilation entrance and guarded loading door. Omar can provide a delivery window if trusted; otherwise examine a posted shift schedule.
2. Approach through a service path, persuaded clerk, or force. Public violence produces a report only if a witness can report.
3. Retrieve the server record. The player can preserve the original, copy an extract or damage the terminal; if damaged, Keira's off-site certified copy arrives later at a cost of time and increased exposure.
4. Rusk initiates lockdown. Escape on foot or in a suitable vehicle; never require driving skill before a driving tutorial.

**Completion:** EV-06 verified or delayed alternative scheduled. **Failure:** checkpoint before lockdown; state rollback preserves evidence integrity. **Tests:** every approach, destroyed terminal, defeat, re-entry, save/load.

### M09 — Terms of Release

**Start:** corroborating record secured or delayed alternative received. **Place:** Ruth's food counter, neutral meeting room, Breakwater approach. **Cast:** Marcus, Ruth, Eli by controlled call, Celia or Lena, optional allies. **Purpose:** let player prepare with relationships and information.

Ruth demands a plan; player chooses whom to trust with the exchange location. Eli confirms his own objective: protect the witness and retain proof; he refuses a plan that knowingly sacrifices Sade. Negotiation with Celia or Lena makes different routes available according to Ledger branch. Darius can provide a distraction only if alive, reachable and willing; another public delivery route always exists.

**ELI:** I didn't keep that file to make myself a hero. Omar was going to take the blame.  
**MARCUS:** You could have told me.  
**ELI:** You were leaving. I didn't want the last thing I gave you to be another reason to stay.  
**MARCUS:** You don't get to decide that for me.

**Completion:** selected plan, explicit promises, ally availability and recipients persisted. **Test:** no ally offers a route based on evidence they have not learned; unavailable allies do not block the mission.

### M10 — Breakwater

**Start:** M09 preparation confirmed. **Place:** container lanes, customs office, exchange platform. **Purpose:** combine observation, dialogue, mobility and fair combat in a dramatic multi-stage climax.

1. Reach the exchange via a known route. A lookout reports visible movement; stealth is possible but not mandatory.
2. Verify Eli is present without requiring a risky pixel hunt. Eli communicates what he knows and acts during the exchange.
3. Negotiate: the player can present corroborated evidence, offer a controlled transfer or challenge Rusk. Celia has leverage over security if the bargain path and her promise remain intact; disclosure provides Lena's external safeguard if she received the copy.
4. Rusk attempts to seize the record. A combat encounter, environmental escape or negotiated withdrawal follows from the selected approach. Combat has cover, telegraphs, a clear evacuation route and checkpoints between stages.
5. Eli and Marcus leave together on a successful main route. A vehicle option is available only after controls were introduced; an on-foot route is viable. Failure retries the current stage.

**Completion:** Eli recovered, EV-06 and recipient states updated. **Tests:** both Ledger paths, zero optional allies, each approach, interruption at each checkpoint, evidence and witness consistency.

### M11 — The Cost

**Start:** Breakwater escape. **Place:** family counter, a neutral evidence handover, garage. **Purpose:** resolve immediate stakes and show persistent costs.

Marcus and Eli discuss the truth without forcing reconciliation. Player chooses an evidence handover supported by the recipients' knowledge and evidence integrity. Outcomes are authored combinations, not a generic colour swap: protected Sade may return to the stall; a broken promise can keep her away; Tessa's support or distance appears at the garage; Celia faces consequences proportionate to corroborated evidence; Rusk's whereabouts and institutional response reflect what can actually be proven.

**RUTH:** Are you leaving now?  
**MARCUS:** I don't know.  
**RUTH:** That's the first honest answer you've given me in weeks.  
*Marcus's phone vibrates. The player may answer, or leave the call ringing as the camera holds on the wet street. The story's state is already committed before this optional final action.*

**Acceptance:** the player understands Eli's outcome, what became of the records, and at least two visible prior-choice consequences. Final duration and branch coverage remain untested.

## 3. Mechanic contracts for implementation planning

### Combat

**Player states:** exploration → combat-ready → attack windup → active hit window → recovery; dodge, stagger, grapple, knockdown and get-up have explicitly defined entry/exit and interruption rules. Animation events are proposals for timing, but damage additionally requires valid range, facing, target and action state. No numerical frame data is approved until a playable test.

| Discipline | Core expression | Risk and counterplay | Slice relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brawler | Close pressure and committed heavy attacks | Range disadvantage; recovery punishes missed heavy | First encounter demonstrates punch/heavy/dodge. |
| Kickboxing | Distance management and combinations | Missed kick creates opening; grappler closes distance | Later archetype test. |
| Grappling | Clinch, control, escape and throw | Exposure to a second opponent; readable escape windows | Later paired-animation test. |
| Drunken Fighting | Feints and irregular timing under reliable controls | Player must read intent; not random input failure | Later authored specialist. |
| Weapon Fighting | Weapon-dependent reach and commitment | Disarm, durability when appropriate, public escalation | Test only after unarmed foundation. |

**Enemy roles:** rusher closes space, lookout seeks help, defender blocks/punishes repetitions, grappler controls range, armed specialist creates a visible ranged hazard. Coordination must be bounded and telegraphed. Debug traces should record attack selection and blocked paths; testers verify a clear response opportunity, not an assumed frame advantage.

**Input:** X punch, Y heavy, A kick, B dodge in combat; B interaction outside combat. Priority: system interruption/pause → cinematic or choice → phone/dialogue → gameplay. Single pointer ownership persists until release; movement and camera support simultaneous distinct touches. No interaction if its state is no longer active when an input is consumed. Required choices cannot be skipped; input layout and buffering require hands-on validation.

### Mission and evidence data

A mission owns stable ID, prerequisite predicates, current stage, completion/failure transition, checkpoint and persistent effects. Choice effects are idempotent: replaying a checkpoint must not pay twice or re-issue a unique item. A disclosure event identifies source, recipient, scope, trigger and time. Narrative content validation should flag references to missing IDs, unreachable mandatory stages, missing recovery path and dialogue requiring knowledge not yet granted.

**Save design:** proposed versioned snapshot contains mission stage, inventory and evidence provenance, knowledge edges, relationships, police identification, branch flags, checkpoint and settings. Save transaction intent → complete candidate snapshot → validate → retain known-good recovery before replacement; exact filesystem guarantees and app-suspension behaviour are unverified. Corrupt saves must not silently overwrite the previous good state. Migrations are tested against saved fixtures before release.

### Phone and interruption

States: idle → incoming → answered / declined / missed → call active → ended / interrupted; message threads and outgoing calls are separate. A call can be protected for an essential dramatic beat or interrupted safely by combat, scene transition or app suspension according to a per-call policy. Preserve unread state and last completed line. If an essential call is missed, a voicemail, callback or authored alternate delivers the essential lead; never silently grant all dialogue knowledge.

### Driving, crowd and police

Driving needs dedicated touch controls, acceleration/brake, steering, collision, camera and enter/exit states; tune separately from walking. Pedestrians near the player need simple routines and reactions; distant representations can use reduced simulation only after continuity and performance tests. Police incidents track severity, witness, identification and report delay; an unreported private incident must not generate omniscient pursuit. Patrol search has readable boundaries and a fair escape; no infinite spawn loop.

## 4. Proposed vertical slice: executable acceptance plan

**MVP for proof, not full release:** M01 from introduction to witness and first confrontation, one authored neighbourhood block, one shop interior, one named witness, one enemy role, phone, two witness approaches, a persistent choice and save/reload. Separate vehicle and crowd scenarios prove their controls and scale without implying they are finished campaign features.

**Recommended implementation order after approval:**
1. Confirm installed Xogot/Godot version and renderer; create backup and baseline project; establish input/scene/state conventions.
2. Build movement/camera and input-priority tests; prove app pause/resume and safe-area layout.
3. Integrate a coherent character/rig/animation sample, interaction and combat hit-state test.
4. Implement authored call, dialogue choices and witness state; save and restore all relevant state.
5. Complete M01 with two routes, failure/checkpoint and audio/subtitle presentation.
6. Add vehicle and crowd test scenarios; profile both target devices and review visual/animation quality.
7. Fix blockers, retest, gather new-player observations and decide whether expansion is justified.

**Entry dependencies:** explicit owner approval of canon and slice plan; confirmed installed app/version; legal source or commissioned assets for Marcus, Tessa, Sade, enemy, shop and lane; a practical rig/animation import workflow; access to target hardware; a release/budget approach sufficient for production. These are dependencies, not claims that assets are unavailable.

**Acceptance matrix:**
- Complete M01 with each witness option and without violence; record player comprehension and defects.
- Defeat/retry, decline/miss a call, skip a cinematic and suspend/resume without skipping a required choice.
- Force-close before, during and after save; restore a valid consistent state without duplicate rewards or debt.
- Hold movement while dragging camera; verify touch separation, safe areas and no combat/UI overlap on both devices.
- With sound off, read all required speech and understand critical sound cues via captions or visual feedback.
- Gather frame-time distribution, memory, loading and thermal behaviour from representative sustained runs. The brief's 60 FPS / 30 FPS aspirations are targets, not achieved results.
- Complete separate vehicle handling and crowd stress scenarios; record spawning artefacts, animation repetition, navigation failures and observed costs.
- Track blocker/critical/major/minor defects with device, build, reproduction and regression status. No progression blocker at slice acceptance.

## 6. Research additions — checked 25 November 2026

- [Godot 4.7: Using NavigationAgents](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/navigation/navigation_using_navigationagents.html), official docs, publication date not stated: avoidance neighbour radius and maximum neighbours influence processing cost and behaviour. **Decision:** test limited nearby avoidance against simpler lane-following, not universal avoidance for every pedestrian. Performance and deadlock behaviour on actual devices remain unmeasured.
- [Godot 4.7: Using NavigationMeshes](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/navigation/navigation_using_navigationmeshes.html), official docs, publication date not stated: a navigation mesh describes traversable space for an agent centre; baking must account for agent radius, and parsing scene geometry at runtime can be expensive. **Decision:** investigate prepared simplified navigation data and test narrow doors and loading transitions. Xogot workflow not verified on device.
- [Xogot: Importing Assets](https://docs.xogot.com/documentation/xogot/importing-assets/), official docs, date and installed build not stated: describes importing assets through the Files interface and mentions glTF/OBJ model import. **Decision:** verify one complete rig, materials and animation in the owner's installed build; general model import documentation does not prove any particular asset's compatibility.
- [Godot 4.7: 3D lights and shadows](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/3d/lights_and_shadows.html), official docs, date not stated: exceeding renderer light limits can cause lights to pop; distance fading and mesh partitioning are suggested mitigations. **Decision:** test a rain/night street for readability and cost; no universal light count or FPS is assumed.
