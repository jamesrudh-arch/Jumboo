# Incoming call — 3D prop and shot study

`incoming_call_study.blend` is an editable Blender 4.2 scene built by `tools/build_phone_call_study.py`. `docs/previews/intro/incoming_call_study.png` is the actual Cycles render. The phone body, screen, buttons, text and workbench are 3D geometry with procedural materials; the screen words are editable Blender text, not generated image lettering. The label **BROTHER** is a relationship label, not an invented name. The owner approved this phone and caller-display direction. It is not an integrated game asset or a representation of a completed intro; the complete call scene and device behavior have not been approved or verified.

The red/green circles are visual details of the phone prop only: **the required opening call will start and be answered automatically, without a mandatory tap**. This still does not show pickup or motion and contains no ringing, vibration, voiced dialogue or playback. It does not prove a working call, legibility on an iPhone, Godot/Xogot import, or performance. The separately approved audio preview remains in `../call_audio/`; it is not embedded in this image or scene.

Rebuild using bpy 4.2 and run `python tools/build_phone_call_study.py` from a suitable Blender Python environment. Rendered in headless CPU Cycles with temporary display-library stubs; not tested in a Blender GUI or GPU renderer. No external model or texture inputs are required.

## Incoming-call motion study — direction approved

`incoming_call_motion.blend` is a separate animation derived from the approved static phone design; the original study is unchanged. `docs/previews/intro/incoming_call_motion.mp4` is a 3-second offline Cycles render at 12 fps with two authored, subtle phone-vibration bursts, a pulsing light and type glow, and a synthesized two-tone ringtone with a low buzz. It is a sound-and-motion study, not a recording of a real device. `docs/previews/intro/incoming_call_motion_frame.png` is a rendered frame. The video has no speech and **does not show pickup**, automatic call connection, the complete cinematic or a game runtime. The white glow is an initial lighting study rather than a verified real phone display. The owner approved this presented motion and sound direction; that does not approve a pickup, connection, completed call scene, or technical/device behavior.

The MP4 was structurally decoded successfully and contains H.264 video and AAC audio. That does not establish perceived audio quality, visual smoothness, mobile playback or device performance; no phone test was run. Rebuild with bpy 4.2, Pillow and `imageio-ffmpeg` installed, using `tools/render_incoming_call_motion.py`. It opens the original scene and produces the separate animation and review outputs. Temporary display-library stubs were again used for headless CPU rendering, not a GUI or GPU test.
