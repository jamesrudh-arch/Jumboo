# Rejected tabletop pickup checkpoint

`table_pickup_checkpoint.blend` is an **unfinished, manually constructed diagnostic**, not an approved intro or game animation. It is retained to avoid losing the actual reach/lift experiment; it does not replace the approved phone-at-ear direction. Its preview is `docs/previews/intro/table_pickup_checkpoint.gif`. The fixed pinch shows a table-to-ear lift, but the hand intersects the table during the approach and the head as the phone reaches the ear. Do not ship this motion.

Run `tools/audit_table_pickup_checkpoint.py` with Blender 4.2 Python to regenerate `docs/previews/intro/table_pickup_checkpoint_audit.json`. At half-frame samples, the phone-local bounding-box check reports about 1 mm nearest-vertex gaps for thumb, index, middle and ring at contact; the pinky is about 10.4 mm away. This is not proof of gripping or absence of all intersections. Hand/table bounding-box intersections occur at frames 2.5 and 3.5. A separate sampled triangle overlap test, using only faces whose vertices are strongly weighted to the head or left hand, reports 0, 225, 395 and 521 intersections at frames 18, 20, 22 and 23 respectively. These tests omit mixed-weight boundary faces and do not establish continuous collision safety.

A further interpolation to the approved ear pose reduced the endpoint head overlap but introduced substantial palm/finger intersections with the phone during the transition. An optimization of the fixed grip instead moved the phone about 48 mm away from the target ear position. Neither variant is accepted. The necessary next step is a physically plausible regrip or suitable commercially licensed captured pickup, with contact and head/table clearance checked across the full motion, followed by visual review and integration with the dialogue and existing staging.
