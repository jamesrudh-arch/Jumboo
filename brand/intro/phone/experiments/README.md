# Rejected tabletop pickup checkpoint

`table_pickup_checkpoint.blend` is an **unfinished, manually constructed diagnostic**, not an approved intro or game animation. It is retained to avoid losing the actual reach/lift experiment; it does not replace the approved phone-at-ear direction. Its preview is `docs/previews/intro/table_pickup_checkpoint.gif`. The fixed pinch shows a table-to-ear lift, but the hand intersects the table during the approach and the head as the phone reaches the ear. Do not ship this motion.

Run `tools/audit_table_pickup_checkpoint.py` with Blender 4.2 Python to regenerate `docs/previews/intro/table_pickup_checkpoint_audit.json`. At half-frame samples, the phone-local bounding-box check reports about 1 mm nearest-vertex gaps for thumb, index, middle and ring at contact; the pinky is about 10.4 mm away. This is not proof of gripping or absence of all intersections. Hand/table bounding-box intersections occur at frames 2.5 and 3.5. A separate sampled triangle overlap test, using only faces whose vertices are strongly weighted to the head or left hand, reports 0, 225, 395 and 521 intersections at frames 18, 20, 22 and 23 respectively. These tests omit mixed-weight boundary faces and do not establish continuous collision safety.

A further interpolation to the approved ear pose reduced the endpoint head overlap but introduced substantial palm/finger intersections with the phone during the transition. An optimization of the fixed grip instead moved the phone about 48 mm away from the target ear position. Neither variant is accepted. The necessary next step is a physically plausible regrip or suitable commercially licensed captured pickup, with contact and head/table clearance checked across the full motion, followed by visual review and integration with the dialogue and existing staging.

## Contact study — rejected by the owner

`table_pickup_contact_study.blend` is a separate follow-up. The owner explicitly rejected its appearance as floppy, generic and AI-looking. **Do not ship or present it as an approved performance.** The hand approaches the phone, closes into a pinch, lifts it off the table, and brings it to the side of Marcus's head. The phone remains bound to the same grip during the lift; the table edge was adjusted to clear the hand. A mocked automatic screen change replaces the incoming controls with a connected state when the phone begins lifting. It is not interactive call logic. The screen faces outward in this grip, unlike the prior approved ear-hold arrangement; do not treat this as a change to the approved direction without review.

`docs/previews/intro/table_pickup_contact_study.mp4` is a silent 3.75-second diagnostic render, made by `tools/render_table_pickup_contact.py` (Blender 4.2 Python and imageio-ffmpeg). No dialogue, full environment, game integration or production lip sync is included. The manually/solver-authored wrist, elbow, finger contact and acting still need visual polish: geometry checks alone cannot make this convincing, and this is not captured or store-sourced animation. This study is not a replacement for the approved phone-at-ear scene or first-call edit.

`tools/audit_table_pickup_contact.py` checks 177 poses at eighth-frame intervals. On this saved scene, the selected head/hand triangles and selected head/hand vertices inside the approximate phone box, selected hand vertices inside the table box, and phone-box corners below the table top all produced zero hits at those samples. The nearest head vertex to the approximate phone box at the final pose was about 2 mm away in a separate measurement. These are sparse approximate tests: mixed-weight faces, between-sample collisions, some sleeve/body intersections and real surface-to-surface gripping are not completely checked. In particular, no absence of clipping or slipping should be inferred for the entire animation. An actual artist review and more complete collision/acting verification remain necessary.
