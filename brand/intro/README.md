# Intro: approved 3D shot study

`cinematic_shot_study.blend` is a packed Blender 4.2 scene, and `docs/previews/intro/cinematic_shot_study.png` is its actual CPU Cycles render. The owner approved this **single framing and lighting study**. That approval does not extend to the full 30-second cinematic, a finished warehouse design, gameplay, or phone performance. It is not a phone screenshot. Marcus is shown in a sampled frame of his source walk, not a new performance. The environment is visibly sparse and needs further detailing, dressing and cinematic direction; the pose and ground contact have not been motion-QA'd. There is no call, animation sequence, dialogue or sound in this still. The game remains unchanged.

The warehouse imagery is a possible later setting; it does **not** change the required order: studio identity, title/reveal, roughly 30-second cinematic, automatic voiced phone call with visible pickup, then warehouse and city. No new story facts are implied.

## Sources

- [Cardboard Box 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/cardboard_box_01), [Steel Frame Shelves 02](https://polyhaven.com/a/steel_frame_shelves_02), [Wooden Crate 01](https://polyhaven.com/a/wooden_crate_01): Poly Haven assets, [CC0](https://polyhaven.com/license). The 1K glTF files came from the third-party [RoboSim mirror at commit `1c39c50ddb8a0043ba2a500485fec687728d8a6d`](https://github.com/rsasaki0109/RoboSim/tree/1c39c50ddb8a0043ba2a500485fec687728d8a6d/assets/props/polyhaven_warehouse). All 15 used input files matched the mirror's pinned SHA-256 manifest; hashes are in `prop_hashes.json`. We did not independently compare the binary files with Poly Haven's direct downloads. Assets were positioned and the shelving rotated; procedural architecture and lighting are original study geometry.

Rebuild with Blender's Python (`bpy` 4.2) and `INTRO_ASSET_DIR` set to a directory containing the three named glTF asset folders and their `.bin` and texture dependencies. `tools/build_intro_study.py` replaces this scene and render. CPU render used headless Blender Python with temporary display-library stubs; no Blender GUI, Godot, Xogot or device test is implied.

## Environment/phone motion framing test — separate offline preview

The owner approved the first-call timing storyboard. To explore how the approved phone-at-ear direction reads under the existing warehouse lighting, `tools/preview_phone_at_ear_in_environment.py` puts the animated character and phone from `phone/phone_at_ear_study.blend` into a separate copy of the previously approved warehouse framing study. Results: `phone/environment_phone_at_ear_study.blend`, `docs/previews/intro/environment_phone_at_ear_frame.png`, and `environment_phone_at_ear_motion.gif`. Existing studies are unchanged. The phone is already in Marcus's hand and the image does not show a table pickup, completed conversation, lip sync, sound or runtime behavior. The resulting environment/motion combination has not been approved as a finished shot.

This is a **lighting and framing experiment only**: it does not establish that the first call happens in the warehouse or change the required sequence (studio identity, title, cinematic, automatic voiced call with pickup, then warehouse and city). The retained camera partly hides the phone behind the hand; the sparse set and foot contact still need work. For example, the lowest evaluated character vertex over the 31 whole-frame samples ranges from approximately 17 mm below to 3 mm above floor height; that is not full sole or continuous collision validation. Phone/grip contact is also unverified. Timing inherits the earlier provisional mocap assumption.

Rebuild with Blender Python 4.2 and Pillow, plus a working Cycles CPU renderer. The script appends only the character/phone elements into a copy of the packed approved framing scene. The saved study and its animated prop are offline research, not a game scene or device test.
