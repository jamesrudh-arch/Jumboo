# Phone motion research — candidate, not an approved performance

The owner found the hand-keyed phone pickup floppy and fake. The approved Marcus appearance and approved call elements remain unchanged; the failed manually posed pickup is **not** production motion. The underlying reasons include a stylized source character, no authored acting or body weight shift, a static face/free arm, and a prop forced to follow an inadequately posed hand. Simply acquiring assets or measuring joint positions does not ensure a convincing performance.

`cmu_80_25.bvh` is an independently researched alternative for body-motion investigation; a separate experimental Blender retarget onto Marcus is described below. [CMU's subject 80 catalogue](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/search.php?subjectnumber=80) identifies trial 25 as **answering a phone** and lists 60 fps. The file is the MotionBuilder-friendly BVH conversion distributed by [una-dinosauria/cmu-mocap](https://github.com/una-dinosauria/cmu-mocap/tree/09a07f54f3bbb58797325f009282d0b2048a2871/data/080), pinned to commit `09a07f54f3bbb58797325f009282d0b2048a2871`; it identifies the conversion as Bruce Hahne's. SHA-256: `e62606b8bf3435eea1aaf6b0aaaa46d7e8b81b63cf2567eb0612b9e461d37faa`. This mirror was not independently byte-compared with a direct CMU or cgspeed download. The conversion README says it inserts a synthetic T-pose as the first frame and changes the advertised frame time; that first frame is **not** a captured acting pose.

[CMU's source terms](https://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/) permit inclusion in commercially sold products but prohibit directly reselling the data, even converted; its homepage also asks for an acknowledgment in publications. CMU explicitly warns that finger and thumb motion was not captured and should be ignored, and that hands/toes may be noisy. The clip therefore cannot solve the phone grip by itself. It is a candidate for body movement only, and must be inspected, retargeted and supplemented with a separately authored, tested hand/prop interaction; whether it actually depicts the desired table pickup is **not yet established**.

## What was verified

`tools/audit_cmu_phone_mocap.py` imports the BVH and unchanged Marcus GLB in Blender 4.2 and writes `docs/previews/intro/cmu_phone_mocap_audit.json`. The BVH has 1,108 frames including its added reference pose and 31 bones; Marcus has 54 bone entries and 21 exact bone-name overlaps. The left-hand/head distance decreases relative to head/hips distance in three sampled frames. This shows the file imports and contains that motion pattern, **not** that the actor answers a phone convincingly, that a hand holds a prop, or that retargeting works. Blender reported zero-length bones on several imported source joints. Exact names are not sufficient for retargeting: [Godot 4.4 notes rest transforms and humanoid alignment matter](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/tutorials/assets_pipeline/retargeting_3d_skeletons.html).

**Timing conflict:** the CMU trial page lists 60 fps, while the BVH file declares 0.0083333 seconds per frame (approximately 120 fps); the conversion README says it applied 120 fps generally. Playback timing must be reconciled against the trial/reference before use. The original full trial has not been evaluated in its entirety or compared against its reference; it has not been integrated or tested in Godot/Xogot/iPhone.

Other routes researched: [Adobe Mixamo FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/creative-cloud/faq/mixamo-faq.html) describes royalty-free commercial game use and Adobe ID access, but the exact needed clip, use terms for the specific asset, availability in this environment and compatibility have not been verified. [Rokoko's free mocap collection](https://www.rokoko.com/resources/download-263-rokoko-motion-capture-assets) advertises commercial use and retargetable skeletons, but no relevant downloadable clip has been verified here. No paid service or unapproved character replacement has been adopted.

## Body-only retarget preview — approved as presented

`tools/preview_cmu_phone_body.py` generates `docs/previews/intro/cmu_phone_body_preview.gif` and `cmu_phone_body_frame.png` from the unchanged Marcus GLB and the BVH candidate. The preview transfers a segment from source frames 100–400, sampled every ten frames, with interpolated half-frames at approximately 12 fps. It assumes the catalogue's 60 fps timing; the BVH header disagrees, so the speed is provisional. The converter's added T-pose is used only as a calibration reference, not portrayed as recorded motion. The target's arms are aligned to the source reference pose for retarget calculations; the approved GLB itself is unchanged.

The owner approved the presented **body-motion preview** as an improvement. This is an actual CPU Cycles render of an experimental retarget, not approval or completion of the pickup, call scene, full motion QA or technical readiness. There is no phone, grip, face performance, audio, table interaction, animation export or runtime integration. Source hand/finger data is not reliable, so finger tracks are not copied. The character's free hand remains open, the feet are not contact-solved, and at five sampled preview frames the overall mesh's lowest vertex ranged from about 14 mm below to 3 mm above the world floor, so floor contact is visibly/technically unverified. The exact measured values are diagnostic only; they are not a full sole or clipping test. Joint mapping, technical motion quality, timing and prop contact need further validation before use. Headless CPU rendering used temporary display-library stubs, not a Blender GUI/GPU or device test.

## Phone-in-hand retarget experiment — not a finished pickup

`tools/preview_mocap_phone_grip.py` reuses the approved body-preview retarget builder, loads a separate copy of the approved phone prop, positions it relative to Marcus's left hand and applies an authored finger pose. It produces `docs/previews/intro/cmu_phone_grip_motion.gif` and front/rear close-ups `cmu_phone_grip_front.png` and `cmu_phone_grip_rear.png`. This is genuine offline 3D rendering, not a game or iPhone capture. The original Marcus GLB, phone scene and approved body-preview output are unchanged.

The **phone is already in his hand at the start**: this does not show him picking it up from a table, answering, or connecting. The screen still shows the incoming state, the phone is visibly away from the ear in some poses, the facial expression is unchanged, and the capture timing and foot contact remain unverified. It is an experiment, not an approved phone performance or complete call. No dialogue, sound or game integration is included.

The accompanying `cmu_phone_grip_audit.json` samples 61 half-frames and compares vertices with over 50% weight in the head and named hand/finger groups against an approximate rectangular phone bounding box. No sampled head or named digit vertices fell inside the box; the `LeftHand` group had up to five vertices inside at three samples. At the contact fit, minimum sampled digit-to-box distances range from about 0.01 mm at the ring group to about 16.85 mm at the pinky group. This is **not** a triangle-intersection test, a complete mesh or continuous-motion check, or proof of a secure grasp. The apparent placement and contact need refinement from multiple angles. Source 60 fps timing remains an assumption because of the documented mismatch in the BVH header.

Rebuild with bpy 4.2 and Pillow using the script above; it invokes the unchanged existing body retarget builder and renders with CPU Cycles. The preview is approximately 12 fps and uses interpolation between source-frame samples. Headless rendering used temporary display-library stubs; no Blender GUI/GPU, Godot, Xogot or phone test was run.

### Additional full-hand capture research

[GRAB](https://github.com/otaheri/GRAB) describes full-body object manipulation with finger and object contact data, but its stated license is for non-commercial scientific research. [ARCTIC](https://github.com/zc-alexfan/arctic) similarly states its data and software are not available for commercial use. Neither was downloaded or adopted for this game. [Handhold](https://github.com/Ozbrett/handhold) is an MIT-licensed grip/arm posing tool that may be worth evaluating against Marcus and the phone; its suitability for this particular pickup and continuous contact has not been tested or established.

[AutoGrip](https://github.com/Jetpack-Crow/AutoGrip) is another MIT-licensed Blender finger-constraint tool investigated for this problem. Its README lists MakeHuman Exchange, Rigify and Auto-Rig Pro as the supported rig types; the current Marcus rig is not on that list. It was not integrated or used to make the contact study, and it does not by itself provide a captured pickup performance.

## Owner quality correction and new research

The owner rejected `table_pickup_contact_study.mp4` as **floppy, generic and AI-looking**. The zero-hit coarse collision audit did not address the animation's weight, timing, posture, acting or hand mechanics; do not ship it or treat it as an approved action. `tools/analyze_pickup_performance.py` samples the wrist bone at eighth-frame intervals in the rejected study's 6 fps scene. The reproducible output `docs/previews/intro/rejected_pickup_motion_metrics.json` reports a peak speed of 0.831 m/s at frame 6.875, acceleration of 26.011 m/s² at frame 7.125, and angular speed of 376.595 degrees/s at frame 6.5. These are finite-difference diagnostics at a particular authored timescale, not anatomical limits, an artistic score or proof of the cause of the owner's judgment. The study has linear key changes, an unconstrained body/hand performance and a prop manually fitted to the grip. A retargeted, properly licensed real interaction with demonstrably suitable hand motion is the next candidate to evaluate, not more arbitrary arm-key adjustments.

### Handhold rig investigation — diagnostic, not a grip or pickup

Evaluated the MIT-licensed [Handhold](https://github.com/Ozbrett/handhold) source at commit `270b74ee4d5c64f8e7d7ac19aea760115d3aba59`. Its documentation says its solver searches palm-based grips and **does not support precision pinches**; fingertip paths are not checked for passing through an object. It therefore cannot supply a captured performance or, by itself, prove the phone pickup is believable. Its full-character solver and static phone grip have **not** been evaluated on Marcus: the headless browser could not launch because the sandbox lacked NSS/NSPR libraries. Its Godot integration is described by the author as not yet tested inside Godot. Do not treat the tool's claims as project validation.

The separate `blender/rig_check.py` was run in `check --quick` mode on the original Marcus GLB (SHA-256 `c41f2bfc62b03a8dd8fbae37d201d4c73bba3806678984c6895f59a7cbf22c4f`); no fix or model change was applied. The tool recognized the humanoid bones and recorded 11 passes, two warnings and three failures: its distance heuristic flagged 37 vertices around the two upper arms, and its stress tests flagged at worst 143 vertices dragged and 338 left behind on RightArm. Full machine-readable values are in `marcus_rig_check_handhold.json`. The tool's own synthetic Blender fixture suite reported `ALL PASS`; that tests its fixtures, **not** these findings on Marcus. The quick check covers selected joints, not every finger or the actual pickup. Its generic bone-axis/range, distance and deformation heuristics may not match this stylized character or its clothing, and the unusually long displayed arm bone tails are already a known concern. These numbers are leads for visual weight/deformation review, not proof of a defect or instruction to automatically reweight the approved character.

Execution used the pip `bpy` 4.2 headless environment rather than a Blender executable. The report was written and the script printed `FAIL` before the Python process returned abnormal status during shutdown; therefore the CLI exit status was not a usable independent verdict. The vendor fixture suite in the same environment returned zero. Reproduce in a normal Blender installation using the pinned source and its documented `node scripts/rig-check.mjs check <Marcus GLB> --quick --report <path>`; compare the actual wrist, elbow, shoulder and clothing deformation in the desired motions before deciding whether any non-destructive rig work is needed. No owner-approved source was changed.
